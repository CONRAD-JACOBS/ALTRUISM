To compare voluntary reCAPTCHA completion between the original competition condition and the subsequently collected no-competition control condition, we fitted a negative-binomial regression with condition as the sole predictor and the control condition as the reference category. A negative-binomial model was used because completion counts were non-negative, positively skewed, and overdispersed. The condition coefficient was exponentiated to obtain an incidence-rate ratio (IRR) comparing expected completion counts between conditions.

Participants in the competition condition completed more voluntary reCAPTCHAs (M = 17.65, SD = 24.16, Md = 11.00) than participants in the no-competition control condition (M = 5.30, SD = 6.12, Md = 4.50). The negative-binomial model indicated that the expected completion count was approximately 3.33 times higher in the competition condition, IRR = 3.33, 95% CI [2.00, 5.56], z = 4.60, p < .001. Thirty percent of control participants completed no voluntary reCAPTCHAs, compared with 2% of participants in the competition condition.

The additional control condition suggests that the substantial voluntary effort observed in the original experiment cannot be explained simply by a general tendency for participants to continue completing reCAPTCHAs when given the opportunity. Participants who received the competition narrative completed more than three times as many reCAPTCHAs as participants who received no corresponding reason to continue. This comparison does not isolate robot-directed helping from every other possible motivational rationale, and the control sample was collected subsequently rather than as a concurrently randomised condition. Nevertheless, it provides a behavioural baseline against which the magnitude of helping in the original condition can be evaluated.
